## 2. Published guidance read
- CASRAI Editorial Board, "How to write a discussion section" (generic): Results report observations without interpretation. Discussion recaps the main finding without every statistic, compares with prior work (agree, diverge, why), states each limitation and its effect, and excludes new results, causal claims from observational data, unexplained citation lists and stock closing sentences. A separate Conclusion is a short takeaway with no new argument.
- Editage Insights (commercial, generic): Conclusion restates the aim and key findings without detail.
- Loevei, "How to write the results" (Open Book Publishers): no interpretation in Results; macro-to-micro order; follow the Methods sequence; do not repeat in text what a table shows.
- HESS review criteria ask whether results support the interpretations and are discussed in a balanced way with related work. Water SA's guide allows "Discussion (or Results and Discussion)" and gives no content rules.

## 3. Synthesis: template for A31E
Only 1 of 8 papers (Ho 2026) has fully separate Results, Discussion and Conclusions; the rest combine R+D but organise it as subsections, one per question, and all Conclusions are short (3-10% of the paper; 250-650 words). The supervisor's separate structure matches Ho 2026 and the CASRAI guide. The sampled papers cite less literature in discussion than the supervisor wants (Prucha none; Ho & Ehret 2025 nearly none), so A31E's target is stricter than the examples; closest models are Ho 2026, He 2026, Simeone 2024.

**Results** (largest of the three)
- One subsection per analysis, in the order the work was done and the Methods describe it (calibration before scenarios, as in Jovanovic and He).
- Each subsection: one opening sentence naming the question and what was done, then values, ranges and figure/table references, then one closing sentence stating the single finding (a number, not a reason).
- No "because", no literature, no "indicates". Report null results too.
- End with a short summary table of findings (as Prucha), which Conclusions can point to.

**Discussion** (about 40-50% of the Results length; Ho 2026: ~0.45, He: 800 words for limitations alone)
- Same subsection order and titles as Results.
- Paragraph build: (1) the finding in one sentence with the figure, no new numbers; (2) the mechanism, stated as a general process and backed by a cited source, worded "X is known to ... (author, year)"; (3) the A31E-specific link, stated only to the extent the A31E data show it, with "may/likely" where untested; (4) the limit of that reading and what data would settle it.
- Literature on other catchments appears only in step 2, as a mechanism or a comparison of magnitude ("within the range of ..."), never as the cause at Klein Maricopoort.
- A closing subsection "Limitations and implications" (He 4.5) holds study-wide limits; limits tied to one result stay in its paragraph.

**Conclusions** (250-500 words, prose; no new citations, no new numbers)
- One short paragraph per research question, in the question order: question restated in a clause, the answer in a sentence, the one qualification that changes the answer.
- Final paragraph: what the answers mean for use of the canal supply and groundwater backup (Ho 2026 closing paragraph). 
- Do not copy Blersch's bulleted list that repeats figures; that is the repetition the supervisor objects to.

## 4. Techniques for orientation and non-repetition
- Same subsection titles in Results and Discussion (see Ho 2026, He).
- Numbers appear once, in Results; Discussion cites them by figure or table; Conclusions give qualitative answers (Ho 2026, He, Simeone).
- Limitation beside the claim it limits, plus one consolidated subsection.
