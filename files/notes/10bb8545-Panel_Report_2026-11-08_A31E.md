# Examination Panel Report: A31E Honours article

**Article:** *Storage, drought and climate change: the reliability of the canal supply from Klein Maricopoort Dam and the role of groundwater in A31E, North West Province* (LH Stroebel), `A31E_Article.pdf`, 74 PDF pages
**Date:** 2026-11-08
**Panel:** Hydrologist (HYD), Geohydrologist (GEO), Methods examiner (MET), Hostile external examiner (HOS), Writing editor (EDI). Chair: Claude.
**Scope:** Whole article, no section focus. Three rounds: independent review, panel discussion, chair's report.

**Page convention:** All page numbers are **PDF pages**. Printed page = PDF page − 5.
**Important limit:** No reviewer could view figures or maps as images, because no PDF renderer was available. All reviewers worked from a full text extraction. Every statement about a figure rests on its caption and the surrounding text. Tables 2 and 3 were garbled in the extraction.
**Abstract:** Left alone, as PANEL_CONTEXT requires. Abstract problems are logged for the final pass only.

---

## 1. Overall verdict

The dam water balance is the strongest part of the article. It closes arithmetically (Table 6), its headline numbers are consistent across the Abstract, Tables 7 and 10 and the Recommendations, and it is unusually candid about its limits. The groundwater, climate and flood components are weaker. Their headline statements outrun what the work shows. The "89% from the rivers" figure and the near-zero figure are largely built into a drain-only, steady-state model that shows no skill over a constant-depth benchmark. The Conclusions say the mean shortfall "can be delivered in both structures", which Table 9 and Section 6.5 contradict. Two compliance problems would draw the heaviest examiner attention. The AI declaration does not say who wrote the interpretation, and the Discussion uses literature from other catchments to explain causes at Klein Maricopoort. Both are fixable without new modelling, and most of the other issues are fixable by rewording or by short additional runs.

## 2. Ranked issue list

Key to the "Panel" column: **Agreed** = no member disputed the substance. **Split** = members disputed the substance or the severity (details in Section 3).

### CRITICAL

**C1. The groundwater backup conclusion contradicts the article's own results.** Section 9 Conclusions (p62), 6.5 (p46–47), 7.6 (p59), Abstract (p6, final pass only).
- Quote: "an abstraction equal to the mean shortfall is small compared with the recharge and can be delivered in both structures" (p62).
- Problem:
  - Table 9 (p47) reports the halved-recharge, halved-K version of structure D at 0.5 Mm³/a as "117.5 (not delivered)". That version fits the four levels equally well.
  - Section 6.5 says the 1.8–2.05 Mm³/a needed during the longest event cannot be delivered in D.
  - The case D mean shortfall (0.70 Mm³/a) was never run in D.
  - Section 7.2 itself says the share of recharge "says little about the availability of groundwater".
  - The research question asks about supply "when the dam is drawn down". The answer uses a steady-state, long-term mean abstraction against mean recharge. Storage is not represented, although GRAII storativities (2.4, p14) were available for at least a storage-depletion estimate.
  - The recharge comparison (1.6–2.8%) uses the 17.336 Mm³/a Reserve figure. Section 6.4 shows that figure is 5 to 9 times the baseflow estimates.
- Raised by: HOS-1, MET-1, GEO-4, GEO-12, HYD-9, HYD-13.
- Panel: **Agreed** (all five). GEO and MET note that the Results are honest. The fault lies in the Conclusion and Abstract wording and in the choice of a steady-state mean as the yardstick.

**C2. The AI declaration does not establish who reasoned the interpretation.** AI Declaration (p74).
- Quote: "Claude then edited the text and extended it to cover the whole project" (p74).
- Problem:
  - The declaration claims only the Introduction and the first Study Area draft as the author's own. It does not say who wrote Methodology, Results, Discussion or Conclusions.
  - The supervisor requires Results and Discussion to be the student's own interpretation.
  - Claude "processed the records and built the dam water-balance spreadsheet", which is the core result. Claude was also "used to check facts and references against the sources", so the tool checked its own work.
  - The author's check of the spreadsheet logic is not described.
  - The signature and date are blank, and the log is only "available on request".
  - Voice drift is visible after the Introduction (EDI-11), for example the chains of parallel "agrees with the finding…" sentences in 7.1–7.3. This is stylistic evidence only, and the panel does not assert authorship.
- Raised by: EDI-1, HOS-9, MET-16, HYD-17.
- Panel: **Split on severity only.** EDI, HOS (raised to CRITICAL in Round 2) and MET rate it CRITICAL. HYD rates it MAJOR from a hydrology standpoint but does not object.

**C3. The water-balance "test" is weakly independent, yet the Conclusions say it "shows".** 5.2.1 (p27), 5.4.1 (p31–32), 6.3 (p40), Conclusions (p61).
- Quote: "A monthly water balance of the dam, tested against the records of 1987–2009, shows that the present dam delivers the registered volume in full" (Conclusions).
- Problem:
  - The inflow is the remainder of the same storage record, so the storage fit (NSE 0.86) is partly built in.
  - The area curve, pan factors and release table were "derived from the test period itself".
  - The rule was then refitted on the fit and test periods together, so all headline results are in-sample for 1966–2009.
  - The monthly canal volume fails the Moriasi criteria. Yet reliability by time, resilience and the 146-month event are all month-based.
  - The yearly canal volume passes only narrowly (PBIAS −22.4% against a ±25% limit).
  - Moriasi streamflow criteria are applied to storage "by analogy" with no defence.
- Raised by: HYD-1, HOS-3, MET-4.
- Panel: **Split on severity.**
  - HYD and HOS: CRITICAL.
  - MET: MAJOR for the method, which 5.4.1 partly discloses, but CRITICAL for the Conclusion sentence as worded.
  - HOS softened "near-tautological" to "weakly independent" in Round 2.

**C4. The groundwater structures: the capture share is built in, structure S is physically rejected, and the 89% figure still leads.** 5.3.2 (p30), 6.4 (p43–45), Table 8 (p45), 7.2 (p56), Recommendation 3 (p60), Abstract.
- Quotes: "Water leaves the model only through drains" (5.3.2); "the least the model allows" (7.2, p56); "the real catchment lies somewhere between the two" (p56).
- Problem:
  - Rivers are drains only. There is no head-dependent river boundary (RIV or GHB), so pumping can only reduce drain outflow, and the S and D capture shares follow from the drain depth.
  - Structure S sends **9.86 Mm³/a** to the rivers (Table 8). That is more than A31E's **total naturalised runoff of 7.21 Mm³/a** (2.3, p12), not merely more than baseflow (2.00–3.43). The article never states this, which all five reviewers called the strongest numerical rejection of S.
  - Structure D (RMSE 8.5 m) is no better than a constant 15.27 m water table (8.6 m) on the four targets. It is worse on the 262 archive levels (16.9 against 14.9 m), where the text says only "no better". Both of D's K values sit on their lower bounds.
  - "Somewhere between" is asserted. No intermediate run exists.
  - The Abstract and Recommendation 3 still quote "about nine-tenths ... from the rivers".
- Raised by: GEO-1, GEO-2, HYD-7, HYD-8, MET-5, HOS-2. The S-versus-total-runoff point is MET-5, adopted by all in Round 2.
- Panel: **Split on severity.**
  - HYD and MET: CRITICAL.
  - GEO (Round 2): downgraded own GEO-1 to MAJOR, because the article admits the drain effect in 7.2. The fault is the Abstract and Recommendation 3 framing.
  - HOS: MAJOR, because Recommendation 3 does say "in one structure".
  - Chair keeps CRITICAL because of the unstated S > total runoff contradiction.

**C5. Literature on other catchments is used to explain causes at Klein Maricopoort, and the cause is stated two ways.** 7.1 (p54–55), 7.2 (p55–56), 7.3 (p56–57), 3.3 (p18), Conclusions (p61).
- Quotes: "These match the two explanations above Klein Maricopoort Dam." (p55); "This amplification agrees with the finding that a change in rainfall is magnified" (p56); "The cause of the low inflow is not known" (p54) against "probably lies upstream" (p61).
- Problem:
  - The following are used as explanations: the Australian Millennium Drought studies (Peterson, Fowler, Saft), De Wit and Stankiewicz (continental), Konikow and Leake (0.85 capture), and Pyburn (dolomite springs) for an inference the article itself calls "inferred from the position of the dolomite".
  - This breaks an explicit supervisor rule: literature on other catchments gives context only.
  - The Conclusions then contradict 7.1 on whether the cause is known.
  - A data artefact (errors in the remainder inflow before 1987) is a third possible explanation that is never addressed.
- Raised by: EDI-3, HOS-6, MET-10, GEO-14, HYD-11.
- Panel: **Agreed on substance.** Only EDI rated it CRITICAL in Round 1. The chair rates it CRITICAL because it breaks a stated supervisor requirement and contains an internal contradiction.

## 3. Points where the panel disagreed

1. **The severity of the circular test (C3).** HYD and HOS: CRITICAL, because the inflow is the storage remainder, the rule was refit on the test period and the monthly canal volume fails. MET: the method is MAJOR, because 5.4.1 discloses it; only the Conclusion wording "tested … shows" is CRITICAL. *Chair:* CRITICAL as a headline claim, with the remedy being the wording plus an honest statement of what was independent.

2. **The severity of the S/D construction (C4).** HYD and MET: CRITICAL; S exceeding total runoff should be stated, and the Abstract leads with S. GEO (own GEO-1) and HOS: MAJOR; 7.2 admits "the least the model allows", and Recommendation 3 says "in one structure". *Chair:* CRITICAL, because the S > total-runoff contradiction is nowhere stated.

3. **Whether the drain depth of structure D is "circular" (GEO-3).** GEO first said CRITICAL. HYD, MET, HOS and EDI, and GEO itself in Round 2, said the 112 levels exclude the 262 comparison levels. It is non-independence in kind, rated MAJOR.

4. **"Bounds" and the threshold (M4).** HYD: CRITICAL, because neither bound is a bound. MET and HOS: MAJOR, because it is disclosed and the fault is in the headline framing. HYD on HOS-4: the aim sentence is loosely worded, but the entitlement threshold is explained (p16, p54), so this is a wording issue rather than substance.

5. **De Wit and Stankiewicz against the elasticity of 2.0–2.5 (M5).** MET: inconsistent, because "10% less rain, half the drainage" implies an elasticity of about 5 while the article says the figures "agree". HOS (Round 2): withdrawn, because they are different quantities (continental perennial drainage against inflow to one dam). HYD: put it as a question, because De Wit's elasticity cannot be verified from the files. *All agree* that a continental result must not be used to explain the local response.

6. **Recommendation 1's "32% to 70%" (M5).** MET, HYD and EDI: a reporting error, because it mixes case D with the rule (0.324) and case M without the rule (0.703); case D unrestricted is 0.635. HOS: legitimate, as the minimum and maximum of Table 10. *All agree* that the threshold change (×1.064) must be stated.

7. **The severity of "reach" (M13).** EDI: CRITICAL in Round 1, MAJOR in Round 2. HOS and MET: MAJOR at most, as a find-and-replace fix. HYD rated it MINOR in Round 1.

8. **Whether the Study Area evaluates maps (M15).** EDI: yes, in the land-cover sentence and the Map 4 caption. HYD (Round 1): the rule is respected. MET: those sentences state data limits and do not evaluate maps, so MINOR. EDI holds MAJOR for those two items.

9. **The severity of the AI declaration (C2).** EDI, HOS and MET: CRITICAL. HYD: MAJOR from the hydrology view, with no objection to CRITICAL under the context rules.

10. **Dam seepage and irrigation recharge (GEO-8).** GEO: MAJOR. HYD: agreed for canal and irrigation return flow, but dam seepage is speculation with no data.

11. **HYD-7's "Class 1 used to avoid calibration scrutiny".** GEO: rhetorical, and motive cannot be verified. The real problem is reporting to 0.01 m in a Class 1 model. Not carried into the report.

## 6. Ten likely viva questions (duplicates merged)

1. Your inflow is the remainder of the same storage record you then test against, and the rule was refitted on the test period. What did the 1987–2009 test show that was not already built in? Which inflow series is the Reference run?
2. Your question asks what groundwater can offset "when the dam is drawn down", but the model is steady state and uses the 74-year mean shortfall. The drought need is 1.8–2.05 Mm³/a for more than ten years. Using your own GRAII storativity, how much could come from storage, and how much from capture?
3. Your Conclusion says the mean shortfall "can be delivered in both structures". Table 9 shows the halved model "not delivered" at 0.5 Mm³/a, and case D's 0.70 was never run in D. Defend the sentence.
4. Structure S sends 9.86 Mm³/a to the rivers, which is more than A31E's total naturalised runoff of 7.21 Mm³/a. Structure D is no better than a flat 15.27 m water table and has both K values on the bounds. Your boundaries are all drains. Why should anyone believe the 89%, and what does "somewhere between" rest on?
5. Where does the 47.5 m weathered-zone base come from, when GRAII gives 25.36 m? What does applying the deposit K over 207.5 m do to the drawdowns? How were the conductances (0.05 and 10) chosen?
6. Why is the registered 2.34 Mm³/a the threshold, when your aim says planning should rest on records, the canal median is 2.02 Mm³/a, and half the register positions lie above the dam? What does "45% to 77%" mean to a farmer?
7. Section 7.1 says the cause of the low inflow is not known, but the Conclusions say it "probably lies upstream". How do you rule out an artefact of the release and evaporation terms before 1987? Why do Australian studies "match" the explanations at Klein Maricopoort?
8. Why only the median and the dry end under one scenario, with annual factors that leave out the September–November signal? What supports "a wetter outcome offers little relief" and "probably understate" without a run? Why is demand raised in the climate cases?
9. A 1:100-year SDF hydrograph, no dam attenuation, no tributary inflow, a 30 m SRTM surface model and no calibration give 1–3% attenuation. Is that a finding about the river, or a property of your boundaries?
10. Which sentences of the Discussion did you write? Walk the panel through the Excel water balance without notes: the seven storage zones, the release table and the pan factors. How did you check the formulae that Claude built?
