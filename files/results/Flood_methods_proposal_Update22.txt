FLOOD RISK ON THE KLEIN MARICO RIVER IN A31E: METHODS AND HEC-RAS OUTLINE
==========================================================================
Prepared 19 October 2026 (Update 22). STATUS: PROPOSAL. The final choice
of method is made with the student after the SANRAL Drainage Manual and
the data in section 4 are in hand. Method details marked [manual] must be
confirmed against the manual before they are used or cited.
 
 
1. THE SYSTEM THE FLOOD MOVES THROUGH
-------------------------------------
- Reach: Klein Marico River from the wall of Klein Maricopoort Dam
  (A3R002) to Kromellenboog Dam (A3R003), 24.7 km, fall about 104 m
  (about 4 m/km) (Section 2.3).
- Catchment at the upper dam 1 157 km² (A31D and A31C); at the lower dam
  1 762 km² (DWS, s.a.c). A31E adds about 605 km² through the Doringrivier
  (joins 4.2 km below the wall, a quarter of A31E), the Waterkloofspruit
  (13.7 km) and the Wilgeboomspruit (at the head of Kromellenboog Dam).
- Upper dam: 7.07 Mm³ over 136.8 ha at full supply (DWA, 2012). One metre
  of surcharge above the spillway crest stores only about 1.4 Mm³, small
  against the volume of a large flood from 1 157 km², so attenuation is
  expected to be modest [to be checked with the volume of the design
  hydrograph].
- Known risk statements: DWAF (2004:170-171) rated the spillway of Klein
  Maricopoort Dam "a major risk" and the spillway capacity of Kromellenboog
  Dam "a major risk factor". News24 (2011) reports that rehabilitation of
  Klein Maricopoort Dam began in August 2009, that the spillway work was
  60% complete in January 2011, and that residents "downstream from Klein
  Maricopoort Dam to Kromellenboog Dam" were warned of possible flooding.
  The present spillway may therefore differ from the one DWAF (2004)
  assessed. [Ask DWS Dam Safety Office for the current spillway data if
  possible; otherwise state the uncertainty.]
- Side note for Section 6.1: the canal volume of 10.57 Mm³ in hydrological
  year 2010, left out as unexplained, falls in the rehabilitation period.
  That the two are linked is only a possibility; it is not to be written
  as a finding unless a source confirms it.
 
 
2. THE METHODS (SANRAL Drainage Manual, 6th ed., Chapter 3) [manual]
--------------------------------------------------------------------
  Method | What it needs | Fit for this reach | Main weakness
  Rational / Alternative Rational | area, Tc, runoff coefficient, design
    rainfall | No for the main river (manual limits it to small
    catchments, about 15 km² [manual]); possible for small side streams |
    lumped coefficient; small areas only
  SCS-SA | soils (SCS groups, already mapped), land cover, design rainfall
    | No for the main river (small catchments, about 30 km² [manual]) |
    small areas only
  Synthetic unit hydrograph (HRU, 1972) | area, L, slope, veld type |
    Possible (medium catchments [manual]) | old regional calibration;
    gives a hydrograph, not just a peak
  Standard Design Flood, SDF (Alexander, 2002) | area, longest
    watercourse, 10-85 slope, MAP or design rainfall, SDF basin
    coefficients (C2, C100) [manual] | YES: built for consistent design
    at this scale; uses data in hand | known to be conservative against
    statistical estimates in some basins; peak only
  Empirical: Regional Maximum Flood, RMF (Kovacs, 1988) | area, K-region
    | YES as an upper envelope | not a return-period flood
  Statistical flood frequency (LN, LP3, GEV on annual maximum peaks) |
    at least about 20 years of ANNUAL MAXIMUM INSTANTANEOUS PEAKS
    [manual] | YES IF the DWS peak records of A3R002 (spill) or A3R003
    can be obtained | monthly volumes cannot be used; dam raised in 1966
    and spillway rehabilitated 2009-2011 (non-stationary record)
 
Records in hand are monthly volumes only. They cannot give a peak. A
statistical analysis is possible only with the DWS annual maximum (peak)
series.
 
 
3. PROVISIONAL RECOMMENDATION
-----------------------------
- Main estimate: SDF for the inflow to Klein Maricopoort Dam (1 157 km²)
  and for the river at Kromellenboog Dam (1 762 km²), for return periods
  of 1:20, 1:50 and 1:100 years, with the RMF as an upper envelope.
- Check: statistical analysis of the A3R002 spill peaks after 1966, if the
  DWS peak series can be downloaded. If it agrees with the SDF within the
  usual spread, the SDF stands; if not, the difference is reported and the
  statistical value preferred, because it is measured at the upstream
  boundary of the reach and already contains the attenuation by the dam.
- Attenuation by Klein Maricopoort Dam: conservatively ignored in the
  hydraulic runs (dam full at the start, peak passed on), with a
  one-paragraph volume check (surcharge storage against hydrograph volume).
- Tributaries of A31E: peak flows added at the three junctions (SDF on
  each sub-catchment), with the note that peaks are unlikely to coincide.
- Kromellenboog Dam: downstream boundary at full supply level; its own
  spillway safety is discussed from DWAF (2004), not modelled.
- Hydraulic model: HEC-RAS 1D STEADY flow (peaks only, which is what the
  SDF and RMF give), mapped in RAS Mapper. Unsteady 2D needs a hydrograph
  and the SRTM 30 m terrain does not justify the extra effort; the
  student's existing 2D mesh can be used as a visual cross-check of
  inundation extent only if he wishes.
- Risk = flood extent and depth against what lies in it: land mapped as
  irrigated (SANLC 2022), the canal (route unknown; note it), built-up
  land, road crossings.
 
 
4. DATA NEEDED BEFORE THE CHOICE
--------------------------------
1. SANRAL Drainage Manual (6th ed., 2013), at least Chapter 3 (flood
   hydrology) with its tables for the SDF basins and the RMF K-regions,
   and Chapter 4 or the table of Manning's n values.
2. The design rainfall file the student already has (design flood peak
   rainfall data), with the name of the program/source that produced it.
3. If the DWS Hydrological Services site is online: the annual maximum /
   peak flow series for A3R002 and A3R003 (and A3H016, A3H030, A3H042 if
   offered), as CSV. If it is offline, say so; the SDF then stands alone.
4. HEC-RAS version (6.x?) and whether the SRTM 30 m tile is the 1 arc-
   second void-filled product (file name is enough).
5. Spillway of Klein Maricopoort Dam: crest length and type, measured on
   Google Earth (date of image), or any DWS dam-safety data.
6. Preference: 1D steady (recommended) or 2D.
 
 
5. HEC-RAS OUTLINE (full click-by-click plan follows in Update 23)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Part A, can start now (independent of the method):
 A1 Terrain: SRTM 30 m GeoTIFF -> Project Raster to WGS 1984 UTM Zone 35S
    (EPSG 32735), bilinear, cell 30 m; clip to the reach with a 2 km
    buffer, from 300 m below the wall of Klein Maricopoort Dam to 1 km
    into Kromellenboog Dam; export GeoTIFF (float32).
 A2 Manning's n layer: clip SANLC 2022 to the same extent; add a field
    N_VALUE by land-cover group (provisional, from Chow, 1959, normal
    values; to be replaced by the manual's table if it differs):
      Main channel (natural, winding, some pools, weeds, stones)  0.040
      Dense bush and woodland                                     0.100
      Open bush / thicket with grass                              0.060
      Natural grassland                                           0.035
      Cultivated (pivots, crops)                                  0.040
      Fallow land and old fields                                  0.035
      Built-up                                                    0.100
      Open water                                                  0.035
      Bare ground / erosion                                       0.030
    Export as a polygon shapefile with the field N_VALUE (or a raster).
 A3 Note the date of the SRTM (February 2000): Kromellenboog Dam shows a
    flat water surface at its level of that month, and the river channel
    itself (tens of metres wide) is narrower than a 30 m cell; the
    vertical error of SRTM over Africa is several metres. These go into
    the limitations, not into the method as corrections.
Part B, after the choice: river centre line, bank lines, flow paths,
 cross-sections (spacing, width), flow-change locations at the three
 junctions, steady flow file (1:20, 1:50, 1:100, RMF), boundary
 conditions (upstream normal depth with slope; downstream known water
 surface = FSL of Kromellenboog Dam), mixed-regime run, checks (critical
 depth warnings, cross-section extent), RAS Mapper depth and extent
 rasters, export to ArcGIS Pro for Map 9 (flood extent and depth with
 irrigated land).
