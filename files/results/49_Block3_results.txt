1. CLIMATE CASES M AND D (grounding; see file 45 of Update 22 for the full evidence)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Region: A31E (about 25.5 S, 26.2 E) lies in IPCC AR6 reference region E. Southern Africa (ESAF), about
1.2 degrees east of the WSAF boundary (Iturbide et al., 2020). Data: IPCC WGI Atlas regional datasets
(CMIP6, 27-34 models per scenario; CORDEX-Africa 21-31 GCM-RCM pairs), land only, hydrological year.
Change for 2041-2060 against the mean of the water-balance record (hydrological years 1937-2009):
  SSP2-4.5: warming p10 / median / p90 = 1.5 / 1.9 / 2.4 C; annual rain -8.5 / -3.1 / +0.5 %
  SSP5-8.5: 1.9 / 2.4 / 2.9 C; -7.9 / -3.0 / +2.8 %   (SSP1-2.6 and SSP3-7.0 in file 45)
Against 1995-2014 (as the Atlas publishes it), CMIP6 SSP2-4.5: spring (Sep-Nov) rain -9.9 % median,
91 % of models drier; CORDEX RCP4.5 spring -5.7 %, 90 % drier. Green Book (Engelbrecht, 2019): >3 C
possible over the northern interior by 2021-2050 under RCP8.5; rainfall change pattern mixed (p. 37).
From rain to the case factors:
  Inflow: rainfall elasticity of inflow to Klein Maricopoort Dam 2.0-2.5 (WR2012 patched observed inflow
    against zone A3B rain, 1936-2009, non-parametric estimator of Sankarasubramanian et al., 2001).
    Median -3.1 % rain -> inflow about -7 %; p10 -8.5 % -> about -17 to -21 %.
  Recharge: recharge ~ rain^1.34-1.39 (MacDonald et al., 2021): median -> about -4 %; p10 -> about -11 %.
  Evaporation depth and demand: +3.2 to 4 % per C (Scheff & Frierson, 2014): 1.9 C -> about +6.4 %.
  Case | Warming | Inflow x | Evaporation and demand x | Recharge x
  M    | 1.9 C   | 0.93     | 1.064                    | 0.96
  D    | 1.9 C   | 0.80     | 1.064                    | 0.89
  D takes the inflow and recharge at about the 10th percentile of the projected rain (the dry end), with
  the median warming. A uniform factor cannot represent the later start of the rains (spring signal):
  this is stated as a reason the cases may understate the loss of reliability.

4. PATHWAYS FROM THE IRRIGATED LAND (MODPATH in GMS, my runs)
-------------------------------------------------------------
Set-up: base runs A31E_GW_S_00 and A31E_GW_D_00 (no wells); 405 particles (9 in each of the 45 cells
under the land mapped as irrigated), started at the water table, tracked forward. Porosity entered as
1.0 in all cells so that travel times scale directly: t(n) = t(1.0) x n. Effective porosity of 0.10 for
the surface deposits (41 of the 45 cells) and 0.01 for the fractured bedrock [source for these values
to be found and verified in the project chat]. Weak sinks: runs made with "pass through" and with
"stop"; the two give identical results (byte-identical endpoint files) - report this in one sentence.
  Structure S: 387 of 405 particles end in a cell whose river drain discharges (the Klein Marico River
    and its tributaries), 18 in cells where only the ground drain discharges; 45 do not leave their
    start cell. Path length median 537 m, 90th percentile 3.0 km, longest 3.9 km.
    Travel time to the river: median 1 404 years at n = 1.0 -> 140 years at n = 0.10 (14 years at 0.01);
    90th percentile about 700 years at n = 0.10; shortest a few months.
  Structure D: none of the 405 particles reaches a river or stream drain that discharges; all end where
    only the ground drain discharges; 288 do not leave their start cell. Path length median 0 m,
    90th percentile 351 m, longest 1.2 km. Median time to the sink 73 years at n = 1.0 -> 7 years at 0.10.
  Reading [INFERENCE, to be worded with care]: in S, water recharged under the fields reaches the river
  after decades to centuries along paths of a few hundred metres; in D it leaves the water table below
  the fields (as seepage/evapotranspiration in the lumped ground drain) and does not reach the river.
  Times are advective only (no dispersion, sorption or decay) and rest on an assumed porosity.
Map 10 layers: 3_DATA/51_GIS_layers_Map10_MODPATH_pathlines.zip (pathlines_S/D, endpoints_S/D; UTM 35S). Do not label times on
the map (the shapefile times are at n = 1.0).

6.1 Design flood at F2 (inflow point of the HEC-RAS reach, 419 697 E, 7 179 054 N, UTM 35S, below the

  162.65 km2), longest watercourse 94.12 km, 10-85 slope 5.36 m/km (elevations 1 155 and 1 533 m), Lc
  27.38 km. Dolomite (Malmani Subgroup) covers 629.6 km2 = 46.4 % of F2 (54.2 % of F1, none in F3)
  (CGS 1:250 000 sheets 2526 Rustenburg and 2524 Mafikeng). Formations: Eccles 262.4, Frisco 206.2,
  Monte Christo 99.2, Lyttelton 61.6, Oaktree 0.3 km2 (3_DATA/49e_geology_dolomite_table.xlsx).
  SDF (SANRAL Drainage Manual, 6th ed., ch. 3): Tc = 16.44 h; T100: point rainfall 161.8 mm, ARF 84.4 %,
  C100 = 0.40. Peaks: 1:2 92, 1:5 289, 1:10 464, 1:20 662, 1:50 974, 1:100 1 252 m3/s.
  Midgley & Pitman: the empirical deterministic FORMULA of SANRAL (2013) 3.6.3, pp. 3-61-3-62
  (Q_T = 0.0377 K_T P A^0.6 C^0.2; Bushveld K_T), not the seven-region curves of 3.6.2; MAP 597 mm:
  1:10 374, 1:20 507, 1:50 703, 1:100 889 m3/s. SDF basin 1 (C2 10 %, C100 40 %; Table 3B.1, p. 3B-2).
  RMF (Kovacs, 1988): K3 1 205 m3/s; K4 2 355 m3/s; the catchment lies on the K3/K4 boundary (p. 3-67).
  The 1:100 SDF exceeds the K3 RMF, and the manual warns that floods from dolomite catchments may be
  less than half the SDF: the SDF is the conservative case, Midgley & Pitman the lower alternative.
