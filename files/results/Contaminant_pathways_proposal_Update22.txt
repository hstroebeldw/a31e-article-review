CONTAMINANT PATHWAYS: EXTENDING SECTION 4.5 TO MEET THE BRIEF
=============================================================
Prepared 19 October 2026 (Update 22). STATUS: PROPOSAL.
 
The Brief asks for a conceptual and/or numerical model "to improve
understanding of ... potential contaminant transport pathways". Section 4.5
now names three routes and shows Map 7 (DRASTIC). It does not yet show
sources, evidence that the routes carry anything, how fast they move, or
who receives the water. A screening of source -> pathway -> receptor,
using data already in hand, closes that gap without transport modelling.

Reading (inference, to be tested in the analysis):
- The two paths of the conceptual model carry two different waters. Water
  from outside A31E, below the upper dam, is three to sixteen times more
  saline than the runoff and groundwater of A31E itself, and Kromellenboog
  Dam lies between them, as a mixture would. This agrees with return flows
  from Zeerust and irrigation above the dam (DWAF, 2004:57, 86).
- Simple mixing check: with 19% of the inflow to Kromellenboog Dam coming
  from the upper dam (Table 15, 1987-2009), an EC of about 55 mS/m from
  upstream and 22 mS/m in the lower dam imply about 14 mS/m for the water
  of the reach, between the Wilgeboomspruit (4) and groundwater (18). The
  numbers are consistent; evaporation in the dam and different periods
  make this a check only.
- Nutrients below the upper dam are mostly low in the median, but the
  95th percentiles at A3H042 (NO3+NO2-N 5.0 mg/L; PO4-P 0.25 mg/L) show
  episodes worth reporting against the South African Water Quality
  Guidelines.
- Points to check: the A3H016 EC values (very low; units/period), and
  where sites 188030, 188038 and 188257 lie (their salinity is the
  highest of all).
 
 
2. PROPOSED CONTENT
-------------------
(a) Section 4.5 rewritten as a source-pathway-receptor account:
    - Sources: return flows from Zeerust and irrigation above the dam
      (salts, nutrients); the irrigated land below the dam (fertiliser
      nitrogen and phosphorus, salts in drainage water); rural settlements
      (on-site sanitation; the Reserve gives 936 people in A31E in 2011);
      road crossings and other land uses on Map 6 [student to confirm what
      the land cover and his layers show: mines, landfill, feedlots].
    - Pathways: the three routes already named, plus floods (wash-off and
      deposition on the flooded land; link to the new flood section).
    - Receptors: the canal and its irrigators (path 1), groundwater and
      boreholes under the irrigated land (path 3), the river reach, which
      is an Ecological Reserve site (Mar_EWR5, present ecological state C;
      DWS, 2021), and Kromellenboog Dam and the scheme in A31F.
(b) Methodology, one short section: screening of the WMS records against
    the South African Water Quality Guidelines for irrigation and aquatic
    ecosystems [DWAF 1996 volumes; to be obtained and cited], comparison of
    the sites along the two paths over a common period, and the mixing
    check above. Travel time of groundwater from the irrigated land to the
    river with Darcy's law, from the model heads and conductivities of S
    and D and a stated range of effective porosity [source to be found].
(c) OPTIONAL numerical step in GMS (no transport modelling): MODPATH
    particle tracking, forward from the 45 cells under the irrigated land,
    in S and D. It shows where water recharged under the fields leaves the
    model (river drains in S, ground drain in D) and how long it takes for
    the assumed porosity. This is the Brief's "numerical" pathway model
    and uses the model as it is. Explicit GMS steps would follow.
(d) Results: one table (sites along the two paths, medians and guideline
    exceedances), one figure (EC and SO4 by site, box plots, ordered along
    the river), and the travel times (and particle tracks, if run).
(e) Discussion: what the two waters mean for the canal irrigators, for the
    aquifer under the fields and for the lower dam; the limit that no
    groundwater-quality series exists.
(f) DRASTIC stays as it is (Map 7), with its caveats.
