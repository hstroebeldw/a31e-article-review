RESULTS OF THE GROUNDWATER MODEL OF A31E (two structures, all cases)
====================================================================
 
Prepared on 11 October 2026 from my GMS runs of the groundwater model
(MODFLOW-NWT), made to APPROVED_Phase_4_Part_1B. Every value is
CALCULATED: a result of my model, not a measurement. Never cite this
file: in the article these are the results of this study. Results of the
model are reported as differences between cases (APPROVED_Phase_1,
section 11), and the two model structures are ALWAYS reported together,
as the two bounds of the groundwater result. Neither is presented alone.
Every shortfall is a shortfall against the registered volume of
2.34 Mm3/a.
 
Units: m3/d in the model; Mm3/a = m3/d x 365.25 / 1 000 000. Residual =
observed minus simulated head (negative: the measured water table is
deeper than the model's).
 
 
1. THE TWO STRUCTURES AND HOW THEY WERE RUN
-------------------------------------------
Common to both: GMS 10.9.6, MODFLOW-NWT (flow package UPW, layer
convertible; Newton solver, head tolerance 0.0001 m, flux tolerance
10 m3/d, up to 3 000 outer iterations, option COMPLEX); well package
with PHIRAMP 0.05 (a well's rate is reduced when the head in its cell
falls close to the cell bottom); one layer of 207.5 m; grid, recharge,
river and stream drains and the two conductivity zones as in the final
model (APPROVED_Phase_4_Part_1B, section 1). Steady state. No other
change between the structures than the two listed here.
 
  Structure S (shallow outflow): the final calibrated model. Ground drain
    1 m below the land surface in every cell. Conductivity: surface
    deposits 0.43036208 m/d, bedrock 0.002900584 m/d (PEST on the four
    static levels, unchanged).
  Structure D (deep outflow): ground drain 15.27 m below the land surface
    (the median depth to water of the 112 regional levels, file 28).
    Conductivity re-estimated with PEST on the same four static levels at
    the same 10 m interval, same limits (surface deposits 0.03-3.0 m/d,
    bedrock 0.0005-0.05 m/d), log-transformed, same PEST mode, two runs:
      run 1 from the values of S: phi 25.641 -> 11.183
      run 2 from bedrock 0.000725146 m/d: phi 19.666 -> 11.183
    Both runs end at the same point with BOTH conductivities at their
    LOWER LIMITS: surface deposits 0.03 m/d, bedrock 0.0005 m/d. These
    are the values of D. The four levels therefore do not determine
    them; the limits do. Both values are upper limits in the sense of the
    design (any lower value would fit the four levels at least as well).
    Transmissivity over 207.5 m: surface deposits 6.2 m2/d, bedrock
    0.10 m2/d (S: 89 and 0.60 m2/d; GRAII mean 22.81 m2/d, file 3).
 
Budget error: 0.00 % in every run (MODFLOW-NWT closes the budget; the
-0.54 % of the earlier PCG2 run is gone).

about as well as the simple benchmark, which shows that the records
constrain the outflow depth more than the conductivities. Because the
status of these levels is not recorded, some may have been measured
during or soon after pumping; this would make the measured levels too
deep and favour D unfairly. The comparison is not a validation.

8. THE SCALED PAIR (no extra run needed)
----------------------------------------
Multiplying recharge, both conductivities and all drain conductances by
the same factor leaves every head unchanged and scales every flow (a
property of the equations; the PHIRAMP threshold depends only on the
layer thickness). An abstraction Q in a model scaled by f gives the same
heads as an abstraction Q/f in the unscaled model. So:
  recharge and conductivities halved, P1 at 0.5 Mm3/a = P1 at 1.0 Mm3/a:
    mean drawdown S 7.43 m, D 117.5 m (D cannot deliver it);
  recharge and conductivities doubled, P1 at 0.5 Mm3/a = P1 at 0.25:
    mean drawdown S 0.96 m, D 4.92 m.
The four static levels fit the halved, the calibrated and the doubled
model equally well, yet the drawdown at 0.5 Mm3/a ranges from 0.96 to
7.43 m in S and from 4.9 m to beyond what the cells can deliver in D.
INFERENCE. This is the clearest measure of what four water levels cannot
decide: the absolute recharge and conductivity, and with them the
drawdown.
 
 
9. THE SCREENING BALANCE (from file 42 and the archive)
-------------------------------------------------------
Mean shortfall against the registered volume = (1 - reliability by
volume, 74 years) x 2.34 x the demand factor of the case:
  Mm3/a          Reference   A      B      C      Stress
  With the rule    0.48     0.65   0.70   0.47   0.79
  Without          0.27     0.41   0.44   0.27   0.51
Rate during the longest event of the Reference run: 1.80 Mm3/a with the
rule (21.86 Mm3 in 146 months), 2.05 without (3.42 Mm3 in 20 months).
As a share of the recharge of 17.336 Mm3/a (DWS, 2021): mean shortfall of
the Reference run 1.6-2.8 %; longest event 10-12 %; the whole registered
volume 13.5 %.
As a continuous rate: 0.27 Mm3/a = 8.7 L/s, 0.48 Mm3/a = 15.2 L/s, the
registered volume = 74.2 L/s. At the median strike yield of 0.3 L/s that
is 29-51 boreholes for the mean shortfall and about 247 for the
registered volume; the strike yields of all 109 boreholes add up to
93 L/s. Scale only: a strike yield is measured while drilling and is not
a sustainable yield; a zero may mean "not recorded".
 
 
10. RUNS THAT WERE NOT MADE (decided on 11 October 2026)
-------------------------------------------------------
Of the 74 runs of the design, 27 were made (plus D_TEST and the two PEST
runs). Not made, with the reason:
- S P1 at 2.0 Mm3/a: read between 1.0 and 2.34.
- D P2 at 2.34 and D P3 at 0.5.
- Recharge x0.72 and x1.20 combined with abstraction (both structures);
  x0.80 with abstraction was made (cases A and B, the main cases).
- The sensitivity set: conductivities x0.25/x0.5/x2, river and stream
  drain conductance x0.1/x10, ground-drain conductance x0.001, outflow
  depth 12.8 and 26.8 m in D. Partly covered by: the scaled pair (section
  8, no run needed), D with the conductivities of S (6.3), and the two
  structures themselves (outflow depth 1 m against 15.27 m).
These are stated as limitations; no result in the article rests on them.
