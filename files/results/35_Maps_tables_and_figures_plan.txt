  From the 1:250 000 geological map sheets 2526 Rustenburg (Walraven, 1981)

  A31E). Shows the four rock groups (quartzite and sandstone; volcanic

  Klapperkop Quartzite Member, Hekpoort Formation), and the mapped faults
  (94 segments) and diabase dykes (13).
  Legend groups, from the rock classes of my layer:
      Quartzite and sandstone           Quartzite, Quartzite/Sandstone
      Volcanic rocks, diabase and tuff  Volcanic rock, Tuff, Diabase
      Mudrock, shale and hornfels       Mudrock, Shale, Hornfels,
                                        Slate/Hornfels
      Surface deposits                  Surficial deposits, Alluvium
      Open water                        Water
  Grouped this way, the shares are 31.8, 24.4, 20.9, 22.5 and 0.4 %, as
  in the text.
  Why: it explains why the aquifers are fractured and low-yielding, and it
  shows that the irrigated land lies on surface deposits in the river
  valley.

MAP 7. Aquifer vulnerability (DRASTIC)  (NEW; in the conceptual model)
  My clipped DRASTIC map of A31E, in the three classes of the national
  map: low (below 90), moderate (90-140) and high (above 140) (DWAF,
  cited by Esterhuyse, 2017). 96.7 % of A31E is low and 3.3 % moderate,
  in a few cells along the northern edge and in the north-eastern corner;
  no cell is high (highest index 124).
  Why: it supports the conceptual account of the pathways along which
  contaminants could reach groundwater, which the Project Brief asks for
  and which is treated conceptually. One map and one paragraph. As with
  the other layers supplied to me, the text does not say where I obtained
  the layer; the classes are cited to the national map. The DRASTIC method itself is explained in one sentence with
  its reference (Aller et al., 1987). It is an index of intrinsic
  vulnerability on a coarse national grid, not a measurement.

FIGURE A. Yearly rainfall at Klein Maricopoort Dam (station A3E003,
  complete years) and in WR2012 rainfall zone A3B, hydrological years
  1967-2008.
  Why: it shows that the one rain station at the dam agrees with the
  regional rainfall on average (583 against 594 mm/a over 41 complete
  years) but not year by year. This is the reason for using the WR2012
  rainfall for the long record.

FIGURE B. Average monthly rainfall and S-pan evaporation at Klein
  Maricopoort Dam (station A3E003), hydrological years 1967-2014.
  Why: it shows the summer rainfall season and that pan evaporation is
  greater than rainfall in every month (about 580 against about 2 000 mm
  per year). This is why evaporation from the dam surface must be part of
  the water balance.

FIGURE C. Average monthly volume in the canal from Klein Maricopoort Dam
  (station A3H031), hydrological years 1997-2014 without 2010.
  Why: it shows that water is delivered in every month (0.12 to 0.22 Mm3
  per month). The water balance takes the seasonal pattern of demand from
  this record and the yearly total from the registered volume.

FIGURE D. Upper panel: monthly storage in Klein Maricopoort Dam, June 1936
  to November 2010 (reservoir record of A3R002). Lower panel: yearly volume
  in the canal, hydrological years 1936-2024 (station A3H031), with the
  registered scheme volume of 2.34 Mm3/a as a reference line. Years that
  are not counted (a missing or gap-coded month, or a doubtful year) are
  drawn in grey. Redrawn in Update 19 so that the grey years are exactly
  the years the counts of Section 6.1 leave out (1975, 1976, 1983, 1994,
  2002, 2008, 2010, 2012, 2013, 2015-2021, 2023).
  Why: this is the central figure. It shows the twelve years in which the
  dam stood nearly empty, and how often and for how long deliveries fell
  below the registered volume.

FIGURE E. Upper panel: yearly rainfall in WR2012 rainfall zone A3B. Lower
  panel: yearly inflow to Klein Maricopoort Dam (WR2012 patched observed
  flow). Hydrological years 1981-2009, each with its long-term mean or
  median as a line.
  Why: it shows that the inflow stayed far below its median for ten years
  after rainfall had recovered. This is the reason the study keeps the
  recorded inflow sequence and does not rebuild inflow from the rainfall of
  each year. The years 1975-1980 are left out because the inflow of those
  years is doubtful. Hydrological years 1994 and 2008 are drawn in grey:
  they rest on patched months that the storage record contradicts. It
  stands in the conceptual model (Section 4.1), not in the Results.

FIGURE F. Upper panel: monthly rainfall in WR2012 rainfall zone A3B. Lower
  panel: monthly flow of the Wilgeboomspruit (station A3H016). December
  1965 to August 1992. Breaks in the line are gaps in the record.
  Why: it shows a stream inside A31E that flowed in every month on record
  until January 1984 and was then dry in 42 of 50 months. It is the only
  evidence inside A31E of how the catchment, and by inference its
  groundwater, responded to the drought that emptied the dam.
