## 8. Recommendations

Each recommendation follows from the Discussion (Section 7) and names its addressees.

1. **Plan for shortfalls that last several years (DWS; Premier's Office).** Drought plans should treat the registered volume as a volume that the dam cannot assure, and should be tested against the dry end of the climate projections and not the median.

2. **Review the yield that the Department gives for the dam (DWS).** The Marico hydrology has not been updated since 1988, and the yield is several times the firm yield found here.

3. **State the restriction rule of the scheme and make it known to the irrigators (DWS).** The rule affects the reliability more than the size of the demand does, and the records do not show which rule was applied.

4. **Do not rely on groundwater as a backup until its effect on the river has been assessed (DWS; Premier's Office; AGES).** The balance of recharge and use says little about the water that can be taken, because in one structure of the model most of an abstraction comes from the rivers that supplied Kromellenboog Dam. If boreholes are investigated, they should be spread over the valley and not concentrated under the fields, which lowers the drawdown in both structures.

5. **Monitor the quality of the water at the head of the canal, at the inflow to the dam and in boreholes below the irrigated land (DWS; AGES).** The canal has not been sampled, the source of the salt in the dam is not known, and no series of groundwater quality exists below the fields, whose drainage would enter the aquifer that was assessed as a backup.

6. **Determine the 1:50-year and 1:100-year flood lines for the Klein Marico River between the two dams, and establish the state of both spillways (DWS; Premier's Office).** The users of the irrigated land on the floodplain need them, and the flood lines need a finer terrain model than the one used here.

7. **Collect the data that would reduce the uncertainty most.** The rule and the position of the water table relative to the river bed cause the widest ranges, so the priorities are:
    (a) The operating and restriction rule of the scheme, with yearly records of the water ordered and delivered (DWS).
    (b) A gauged record of the inflow to Klein Maricopoort Dam and of the water used above it, to settle the cause of the low inflow (DWS).
    (c) Water levels and electrical conductivity in boreholes beside the Klein Marico River below the dam (DWS; AGES).
    (d) The lithological logs of the valley deposits in the archive, and pumping tests in those deposits (AGES).
    (e) Water-level series in the valley and in the western arm (AGES).

## 9. Conclusions

This study quantified the reliability of the canal supply from Klein Maricopoort Dam, the part that groundwater inside A31E could play, and the effect of climate change, so that drought planning can rest on the records and not on the registered volume. Most results are ranges, because the records leave key points open.

The canal fell short of the registered volume in most of the years that could be counted. One drought of many years is the main feature of the record. The inflow to the dam stayed low in it although the rainfall recovered, and the cause has not been settled. Most of the water that reached Kromellenboog Dam in the drought was generated between the dams, but it enters the river below the canal and could not replace the canal supply.

A water balance of the dam, which reproduces its storage but is not independent of the records, shows that the restriction rule affects the reliability more than the demand does. The rule is not known.

The groundwater model shows that the shortfall is small against the recharge, but not how much water can be taken. In one structure most of the pumped water would come from the rivers, and in the other the pumped cells could not deliver the larger rates. The water levels available cannot decide between the structures, so the model cannot show whether the water would be new or the river's own.

Under the warmer and probably somewhat drier conditions projected for about 2050, the canal supply weakens, most at the dry end of the projections, and so does the groundwater backup in one structure. These cases probably understate the loss.

Part of the irrigated land lies on the floodplain. The size of the design flood depends on the method, and the flooded extent is a screening and not a flood line. The river below the dam is above the salinity target for irrigation in most samples, and the runoff of A31E is fresher. Water from under the irrigated land would reach a river in one structure and not in the other, and the drainage of the fields would reach the aquifer that was assessed as a backup.

The registered volume cannot be assured by the dam, and groundwater inside A31E cannot yet be relied on as a backup, because its effect on the river has not been established. Both weaken under the climate cases. Each range has a cause that data could narrow, and the recommendations follow from that.
