## 8. Recommendations

Each recommendation follows from the Discussion (Section 7) and names its addressees.

1. **Plan for shortfalls that last several years (DWS; Premier's Office).** Drought plans should treat the registered volume as a volume that the dam cannot assure, and should be tested against the dry end of the climate projections and not the median.

2. **Review the yield that the Department gives for the dam (DWS).** The report that gives the yield states no method, and the Marico hydrology has not been updated since 1988. The historic firm yield found here is well below it.

3. **State the restriction rule of the scheme and make it known to the irrigators (DWS).** The records do not show which rule was applied, and the rule sets the balance between how often and how badly the canal fails.

4. **Do not rely on groundwater as a backup until its effect on the river has been assessed (DWS; Premier's Office; AGES).** In one structure of the model most of an abstraction comes from the river that supplies Kromellenboog Dam.

5. **Monitor the quality of the river below the dam, the inflow to the dam and boreholes below the irrigated land (DWS; AGES).** The river below the dam is above the salinity target for irrigation in most samples, and the source of its salt and the quality of the groundwater below the fields are not known.

6. **Determine the 1:100-year flood line for the Klein Marico River between the two dams (DWS; Premier's Office).** Users of the irrigated land on the floodplain need it, and it needs a finer terrain model than the one used here.

7. **Collect the data that would reduce the uncertainty most.** The restriction rule and the position of the water table relative to the river bed decide most of the ranges, so the priorities are:
    (a) The operating and restriction rule of the scheme, with yearly records of the water ordered and delivered (DWS).
    (b) A gauged record of the inflow to Klein Maricopoort Dam and of the water used above it, to settle the cause of the low inflow (DWS).
    (c) Water levels and electrical conductivity in boreholes beside the Klein Marico River below the dam (DWS; AGES).
    (d) The lithological logs of the valley deposits in the archive, and pumping tests in those deposits (AGES).
    (e) Water-level series in the valley and in the western arm (AGES).

## 9. Conclusions

This study set out to quantify the reliability of the canal supply from Klein Maricopoort Dam, how far groundwater within A31E could offset its shortfalls and how both respond to a drier climate, and to screen the floods and the contaminant paths along the irrigated reach.

The canal supply is not reliable for the registered volume. The dam is small against it, and one drought of many years, in which the inflow stayed low, left the canal short year after year. Groundwater within A31E cannot yet be relied on as a backup. In one structure of the model most of the pumped water would come from the river that feeds Kromellenboog Dam, and the water levels available cannot show which structure is closer to the catchment. In the sensitivity cases for about 2050, the canal supply and the discharge of groundwater to the rivers are both lower, most in the dry case.

Part of the irrigated land lies on the floodplain and is covered by the 1:100-year flood. Water from under the irrigated land would reach a river in one structure and not in the other. The river below the dam is above the salinity target for irrigation in most samples, and the water of A31E is fresher.

The registered volume cannot be assured by the dam, and the recommendations follow from this: plan for droughts of several years, review the yield, state the restriction rule, and collect the data that would narrow the two main unknowns.
