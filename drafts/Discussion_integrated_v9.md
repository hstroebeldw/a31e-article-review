## 7. Discussion

The canal supply cannot be relied on for the registered volume. The dam is small against that volume, so a drought of several years leaves it empty, and the way deliveries are restricted as it empties sets the balance between how often and how badly the supply fails (Hashimoto et al., 1982:18–19). Why the inflow stayed low in the drought is not settled. The historic firm yield of the dam is well below the 1.4 Mm³/a that the Department gives, a figure for which the report states no method and which rests on hydrology that has not been updated since 1988 (DWAF, 2004:50, 54, 83).

The drought also shows that A31E carries two waters. The water in the canal comes from outside the catchment, fell short in the drought and in most samples is above the salinity target for irrigation. The Wilgeboomspruit and the groundwater of A31E are much fresher, but the water of A31E enters the river below the canal and does not reach it.

Groundwater is tied to both waters, so it can back up the canal only in part. Pumped water often comes from less discharge elsewhere (Leake & Barlow, 2013). In structure S it comes mostly from the Klein Marico River and so from the water that feeds Kromellenboog Dam. Which structure is closer to the catchment the records cannot show: the 262 other water levels favour structure D on the share within 10 m, but neither structure fits them better than a constant depth to water. The same question decides where water from the irrigated fields goes. In structure S nearly all of it would reach a river, and in structure D none would.

In the two sensitivity cases for about 2050, the canal supply is less reliable than in the Reference run, most in the dry case, and the discharge of groundwater to the rivers and streams falls by about as much as the recharge in both structures. Planning should therefore be tested against the dry end of the projections and not the median.

Part of the irrigated land lies on the floodplain, so its users face two risks, a shortage that lasts for years and flooding. The model shows that about a quarter of the irrigated land in the strip is covered by the 1:100-year flood, and the flood line itself still has to be determined.

For the catchment, this means that planning should assume droughts of several years and not the registered volume, and that groundwater cannot yet be relied on as a backup, because its effect on the river has not been established. Each range has a cause that data could narrow: the restriction rule of the canal, and the position of the water table relative to the river bed for groundwater.
