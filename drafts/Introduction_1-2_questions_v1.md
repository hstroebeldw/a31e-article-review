## 1.2 Aim, objectives and research questions

The aim of this study is to quantify the reliability of the canal supply from Klein Maricopoort Dam, the extent to which groundwater within A31E could offset its shortfalls and the response of both to climate change, so that drought planning in the catchment can rest on the records rather than on a registered volume. The study is a desktop assessment of existing records, intended for the Department of Water and Sanitation (DWS), the North West Premier's Office and the consulting partner AGES. Flood risk along one reach of the Klein Marico River and the pathways along which contaminants could move are screened, and the transport of contaminants is not modelled.

The objectives of the study are:

1. To compile and review the existing hydrological, geohydrological and water-use information for A31E, identify its gaps, and develop a conceptual model of how water moves through the catchment.

2. To simulate the water balance of Klein Maricopoort Dam and its canal and quantify the reliability of supply against the registered scheme volume.

3. To assess with a groundwater flow model how far abstraction within A31E could offset shortfalls in the canal supply, and the limits that the data place on this assessment.

4. To assess the effects of climate change by about 2050 on the reliability of the canal supply and on groundwater.

5. To screen the flood risk along the irrigated reach of the Klein Marico River and the pathways along which contaminants from the land could reach the rivers.

6. To provide evidence-based recommendations, including priorities for data collection, to the DWS, the North West Premier's Office and AGES.

The research questions are:

1. How reliable is the canal supply from Klein Maricopoort Dam to irrigators in quaternary catchment A31E under the historical sequence of wet and dry years, to what extent could groundwater within A31E offset the shortfalls that arise when the dam is drawn down, and how would both change under plausible climate conditions for about 2050?

2. How large are the floods along the irrigated reach, and along which paths could contaminants from the land reach the receptors?
