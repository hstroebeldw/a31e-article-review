## 6.4 Groundwater model: fit and water budget

The calibration gave different conductivities in the two structures (Table 10). In structure D both ended at the lower limits of their ranges, and the four static levels do not determine them, since any lower value would fit the levels at least as well. Structure S matches three of the four levels within 6 m, but it misses borehole 2526AD00180 in the valley by 28.9 m. The fit of structure D at the four levels comes from the depth of its ground drain and not from its conductivities. With the regional recharge, structure S fits only with a bedrock transmissivity of 0.60 m²/d, and structure D reaches its fit at the lower limit of 0.10 m²/d, both far below the regional 22.81 m²/d.

Of the 262 archive levels that were not used in the calibration, structure D has twice as many within 10 m as structure S, but it fits slightly worse than the benchmark (Table 10; Figure 12). On the lowest ground the measured water table is deeper than that of both structures, by twice as much in structure S. Structure S places the water table within 5 m of the surface at many more boreholes than the nine where the levels are that shallow. The status of these levels is not recorded, so some may have been measured during pumping. Under the irrigated land, the depth to water is 4.5 m in structure S and 15.3 m in structure D (Map 8).

The two structures receive the same recharge but lose it by different routes (Table 10). The discharge to the rivers and streams in structure S exceeds every estimate of baseflow, 2.00 to 3.43 Mm³/a (DWS, 2021; Bailey & Pitman, 2016), and the net contribution of the reach (Table 7). In structure D it is below every estimate. The comparison is of scale only, as the quantities are of different kinds. Halving or doubling the recharge, the conductivities and the drain conductances together leaves the water levels unchanged and scales the flows in proportion, so that structure S still exceeds the estimates at half (4.93 Mm³/a) and structure D remains below them at double (1.26 Mm³/a).

The Reserve determination gives a groundwater use of 0.81 Mm³/a against the 0.285 Mm³/a registered, and a baseflow of 2.00 Mm³/a (DWS, 2021). This leaves 14.5 Mm³/a of its recharge of 17.336 Mm³/a unaccounted for, which is five to nine times the estimates of baseflow. The recharge of the model, 17.53 Mm³/a, is slightly more.

## 6.5 Abstraction to offset the shortfall

In structure S, the mean drawdown in the 45 pumped cells is 2.30 m at 0.5 Mm³/a and 40.8 m at the registered volume of 2.34 Mm³/a, at which the water level in 19 of the cells is deeper than 47.5 m, the base of the weathered zone (Figure 13). At the regional recharge, the drawdown in structure D is five to nine times larger at 0.25 and 0.5 Mm³/a, and any lower conductivity would give more. From 1.0 Mm³/a the pumped cells of structure D cannot deliver the rate asked, and they yield at most about 1.2 Mm³/a. The drawdowns are means over cells of 200 m, and the drawdown in a pumped borehole would be larger.

The source of the pumped water is opposite in the two structures. In structure S, 87% to 89% of the pumped water comes from less discharge to the rivers and streams at rates up to 1.0 Mm³/a, and more than three-quarters at the registered volume. At 0.5 Mm³/a, about 69% comes from the Klein Marico River itself and 20% from the lower Waterkloofspruit. In structure D, the rivers and streams supply under 2%, and the rest comes from the ground drain.

Table 11 lists the cases that each change one part of the set-up. With the conductivities of structure S, the drawdown in structure D falls to about that of structure S, but its water still comes entirely from the ground drain. Spreading the abstraction over the surface deposits within 1 km reduces the mean drawdown to a quarter in structure S and to one twenty-eighth in structure D, without changing the source, whereas abstraction from the bedrock within 1 km lowers the share from the rivers and streams in structure S to 58%. The halved and doubled models fit the four water levels equally well, and their drawdowns in structure S differ by a factor of about eight. In structure D the halved model delivers about 93% of the rate.

## 7.2 Groundwater as a backup

The shortfall is a small share of the recharge, but this says little about the water that can be taken, because in the long term pumped water often comes from less discharge elsewhere (Leake & Barlow, 2013). In the model, the water table of structure S stands above the river beds, so that pumping under the irrigated land takes mostly water that would otherwise have flowed down the river to its users and to Kromellenboog Dam. In structure D the water table stands below the river beds, so that the rivers lose almost nothing, but the pumped cells cannot deliver the larger rates. In these runs, and partly through the way the drains are built, the depth of the ground drain decided where the pumped water came from, and the conductivity decided how far the water level fell.

The records cannot show which structure is closer to the catchment. The baseflow estimates lie between the two structures, but on the lowest ground the measured water table is deeper than in either, so neither agrees with both. Calibrated models can differ greatly in their predictions when their structure differs (Troldborg et al., 2007), and four water levels cannot separate the recharge from the conductivity (Knowling & Werner, 2016). The results are a range and not a prediction.

Two further limits apply. The model removes water only through its drains, so it cannot show pumping drawing water from a river into the aquifer (Leake & Barlow, 2013), and the share taken from the rivers is a minimum in both structures. The model is also at steady state, so it does not show the first period of pumping, when most of the water comes from the aquifer itself (Leake & Barlow, 2013).
