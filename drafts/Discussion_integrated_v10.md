## 7. Discussion

The canal supply from Klein Maricopoort Dam cannot be relied on for the registered volume. The dam is small in relation to that volume, so a drought of several years empties it, and the manner in which deliveries are restricted as it empties sets the balance between how often the supply fails and how severe a failure is (Hashimoto et al., 1982:18–19). Why the inflow remained low during the drought is not yet settled. The historic firm yield of the dam is also well below the 1.4 Mm³/a that the Department gives, a figure for which the report states no method and which rests on hydrology that has not been updated since 1988 (DWAF, 2004:50, 54, 83).

The drought further shows that A31E carries two waters, which differ in origin and in quality. The water in the canal comes from outside the catchment; it fell short during the drought, and in most samples its salinity is above the target for irrigation. The Wilgeboomspruit and the groundwater of A31E are much fresher, but this water enters the river below the canal and therefore does not reach it.

Because groundwater is tied to both waters, it can back up the canal only in part. Water pumped from an aquifer often comes from less discharge elsewhere (Leake & Barlow, 2013). In structure S it comes mostly from the Klein Marico River, and so from the water that feeds Kromellenboog Dam. Which structure is closer to the catchment the records cannot yet show: the 262 other water levels favour structure D on the share within 10 m, but neither structure fits them better than a constant depth to water. The same question decides where water from the irrigated fields goes, since in structure S nearly all of it would reach a river, whereas in structure D none would.

Climate change adds to these limits. In the two sensitivity cases for about 2050, the canal supply is less reliable than in the Reference run, most of all in the dry case, and in both structures the discharge of groundwater to the rivers and streams falls by about as much as the recharge. Planning should therefore be tested against the dry end of the projections rather than against the median.

For part of the irrigated land, shortage is not the only risk. This land lies on the floodplain, so its users face both a shortage that can last for years and flooding. The model shows that about a quarter of the irrigated land in the strip is covered by the 1:100-year flood, while the flood line itself still has to be determined.

Taken together, planning in the catchment should assume droughts of several years rather than the registered volume, and groundwater cannot yet be relied on as a backup, because its effect on the river has not been established. Each remaining range has a cause that data could narrow: for the canal, the restriction rule, and for groundwater, the position of the water table relative to the river bed.
