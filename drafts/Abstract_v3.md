## Abstract

Irrigation in quaternary catchment A31E, North West Province, depends on a canal from Klein Maricopoort Dam, which carried less than the registered volume of 2.34 Mm³/a in thirteen successive years from 1984. This desktop study assessed how reliable the canal supply is, how far groundwater within A31E could offset its shortfalls and how both respond to a drier climate, and it screened the floods and contaminant paths along the irrigated reach. A monthly water balance of the dam (1936–2009) was run with and without a restriction rule, and a steady-state groundwater model was run in two structures that differ in the depth of outflow. With the rule, the canal delivers the registered volume in 45% of the years, and without restriction in 77%. In one structure, pumped groundwater would come mostly from the river that feeds Kromellenboog Dam. In the sensitivity cases for about 2050, the reliability of the canal is lower, most in the dry case. The 1:100-year flood, estimated with the SDF method, covers about a quarter of the irrigated land in the strip below the dam, and the modelled reach reduces its peak by only 1% to 2%. The river below the dam is above the salinity target for irrigation in most samples. The registered volume cannot be assured by the dam, and groundwater cannot yet be relied on as a backup, because its effect on the river has not been established.

**Keywords:** canal supply reliability; reservoir water balance; multi-year drought; groundwater flow model; capture; climate change; design flood; particle tracking
