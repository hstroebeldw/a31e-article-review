## 6.6 Climate change

The two climate cases of Table 5 were run through the water balance of the dam and through the groundwater model, to see how much of the canal supply and of the groundwater would be lost if the climate follows the median of the projections (case M) or their dry end (case D). Table 10 gives the results against the Reference run.

With the rule, the reliability by time falls from 0.59 in the Reference run to 0.54 in case M and 0.48 in case D. Without restriction it falls from 0.87 to 0.83 and 0.79. Without restriction the scheme would receive its full yearly volume in about three of every four years in the Reference run, about seven in ten years in case M and fewer than two in three years in case D. The mean shortfall rises by a quarter to a third in case M and by a half to two-thirds in case D. With the rule, case D is 6 points below case M, which is more than case M is below the Reference run. In case D the longest event also grows from 146 to 163 months, and the months with less than half of the registered volume increase from 156 to 214.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reference | 0.590 / 0.867 | 0.446 / 0.770 | 0.48 / 0.27 | 146 / 20 | 9.86 Mm³/a | 0.63 Mm³/a |
| M (median) | 0.541 / 0.829 | 0.405 / 0.703 | 0.60 / 0.36 | 148 / 35 | −3.8% | −5% |

The storage of the dam is also lower at all times, and not only in droughts (Figure 14). With the rule, the dam holds at least half of its capacity in about 64% of the months in the Reference run, in about 59% in case M and in about 53% in case D.

**Figure 14** (unchanged): Share of months in which the simulated storage of Klein Maricopoort Dam is at or above each level, for the Reference run and cases M and D, hydrological years 1936–2009. Left panel: with the restriction rule; right panel: without restriction.

In the groundwater model, the discharge to the rivers and streams falls by about as much as the recharge. In structure S it falls by 3.8% and 10.5%, slightly less than the recharge (4% and 11%), and in structure D by 5% and 14%, more than the recharge. Map 7 shows how much lower the water table stands in case D than in the Reference run. Both runs include an abstraction of 0.5 Mm³/a under the irrigated land, so the lowering shown is that of the drier climate alone. In structure S the mean lowering is 1.00 m, and it exceeds 1 m over 220 of the 602 km², mainly on the high ground between the valleys. In the valleys the drains hold the water table, and under the pumped cells the lowering averages 0.23 m. In structure D the mean lowering is 0.38 m, and it is small over most of the catchment, but under the pumped cells it averages 3.78 m, and the drawdown of the abstraction increases from 20.6 to 24.3 m. Under case M the lowering is about a third of that under case D.

## 6.7 Flood risk in the modelled reach

The design flood at F2 was estimated with three methods (Section 5.4.3), and four plans were routed through the 15.7 km reach with the 2D model, to see how large the floods are and how much of the cultivated land they cover.

The three methods differ widely (Table 11). At the 1:100-year return period the SDF gives 1 252 m³/s and the Midgley and Pitman formula 889 m³/s, which is 71% of the SDF peak, so the SDF peak is 41% higher. The SDF peak also exceeds the RMF of region K3 (1 205 m³/s). The catchment above the reach is 46.4% dolomite of the Malmani Subgroup, all of it above Klein Maricopoort Dam, and A31E itself contains none (Michaluk & Keyser, 1991; Walraven, 1981).

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1:50, SDF | 974 | 972 / 951 | 2.1% | 7.72 | 6.26 | 3.17 |

Over the reach the peak decreases by 1% to 3% and arrives about two hours later, because the floodplain holds at most 14% to 16% of the flood volume. The flooded area changes much less than the peak. The 1:100-year SDF peak is 29% larger than the 1:50-year peak, but it floods only 3% more land, and the Midgley and Pitman flood has a peak 29% lower than the SDF flood and covers 5% less land. The outlines of the 1:50-year and 1:100-year SDF floods almost coincide on Map 9. The 1:100-year flood covers 0.25 km² more land, which along the two edges of the 15.7 km reach moves each edge outwards by about 8 m on average, less than a third of one 30 m cell of the terrain. The water surface of the 1:100-year flood stands a median of 0.28 m higher than that of the 1:50-year flood where both are wet. The full shallow-water equations change the flooded area by 5% and the outflow peak by 0.2%.

In every plan 3.1 to 3.3 km² of land mapped as cultivated is flooded, which is about 40% of the cultivated land in the model area, and most of it lies under more than 1 m of water. This includes about 44 ha of the land mapped as irrigated under the 1:100-year SDF flood, of which 38 ha lies more than 1 m deep (42 to 46 ha across the plans).

## 6.8 Pathways and the two waters

Two things were examined. Particles were released from the 45 model cells under the irrigated land and tracked forward in both model structures (MODPATH; Pollock, 2016), to see where water that recharges under the fields would go. The records of the Department were screened to compare the water of the river below the dam with the water generated inside A31E.

In structure S, 387 of the 405 particles (96%) end in a cell where a river drain discharges (Map 10). Their paths have a median length of 537 m, and none is longer than 3.9 km. At an effective porosity of 0.10, the median time to reach the river is 140 years and the 90th percentile about 700 years, while paths from cells beside the river take a few months. In structure D, no particle reaches a river or stream, and 288 of the 405 do not leave the cell in which they started, because the water table under the fields stands 15.3 m deep, below the river beds.

**Map 10** (unchanged): Flow paths of 405 particles released in the 45 model cells under the irrigated land (MODPATH 7; Pollock, 2016), for (a) structure S and (b) structure D. Yellow dots mark the end of each path. In S, 387 of the 405 paths end at a river; in D none do. The paths show advective flow direction only; contaminant transport is not modelled, and travel times depend on an assumed porosity.

The records of the Department show that the two paths carry different water (Figure 15; Table 12). Below Klein Maricopoort Dam, the median EC was 51.2 mS/m in 1989–1993 and 62.3 mS/m in 2012–2023, and 86% to 99% of the samples exceeded the upper limit of the target range for irrigation, 40 mS/m. The three sites along the reach had higher medians of 76 to 83 mS/m in 2004–2007, based on one to three samples each from a different period. The Wilgeboomspruit, which carries water generated inside A31E, had a median of 3.85 mS/m and no exceedance, and the groundwater of A31E has a median of 17.7 mS/m, based on seven samples (DWS, 2021:163). Kromellenboog Dam, which receives both waters, lies between them at 21.7 mS/m. Sulphate follows the same order, with medians of 30 to 57 mg/L below the upper dam and 102 to 145 mg/L along the reach, against 17.7 mg/L in Kromellenboog Dam and 3.05 mg/L in the Wilgeboomspruit. No sample at any site exceeds the guidelines for chloride, sodium or SAR.

**Figure 15** (unchanged) and **Table 12** (unchanged), as in the current article.

Over the common period of 2012–2023, the river below the upper dam had a median EC of 62.3 mS/m and Kromellenboog Dam 20.7 mS/m. With 19% of the inflow to the lower dam coming from the upper dam, a simple mixing check gives an EC of about 11 mS/m for the water of the reach, which lies between that of the Wilgeboomspruit and that of the groundwater. The nutrients do not separate the two paths in the same way. Phosphate exceeds 0.025 mg/L in 19% of the samples below the upper dam, in 29% in Kromellenboog Dam and in 20% in the Wilgeboomspruit.

The DRASTIC index is low over 96.7% of A31E and moderate over 3.3% (Map 11; DWAF, cited by Esterhuyse, 2017).
