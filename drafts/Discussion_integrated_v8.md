## 7. Discussion

The canal supply cannot be relied on for the registered volume. The dam is small against that volume, so a drought of several years leaves it empty, and the way deliveries are restricted as it empties matters more for the reliability than the size of the demand (Hashimoto et al., 1982:19). Without restriction the firm yield is only about a quarter of the 1.4 Mm³/a that the Department gives, a figure that rests on hydrology not updated since 1988 (DWAF, 2004:50, 83).

The drought also shows that A31E carries two waters. The water that the dam releases comes from outside the catchment, failed in the drought, and in most samples is above the salinity target for irrigation. The runoff and groundwater of A31E are much fresher, but they enter the river below the canal and could not replace the supply.

Groundwater is tied to both waters, so it can back up the canal only in part. Pumped water often comes from less discharge elsewhere (Leake & Barlow, 2013). In structure S it comes mostly from the river that feeds Kromellenboog Dam, and in structure D the pumped cells cannot deliver the larger rates. The records cannot show which structure is closer to the catchment. The same unknown decides where water from the irrigated fields goes: in structure S nearly all of it would reach a river, and in structure D none would.

A warmer and probably somewhat drier climate weakens the canal supply and, in structure D, the groundwater, so planning should be tested against the dry end of the projections and not the median.

Part of the irrigated land lies on the floodplain, so its users face two risks, a shortage that lasts for years and flooding. The size of the design flood depends mainly on the method, and the dolomite above the dam may make the SDF too high (SANRAL, 2013:3-45). The state of the spillways of both dams is not known to this study.

For the catchment, this means that planning should assume droughts of several years and not the registered volume, and that groundwater cannot yet be relied on as a backup, because its effect on the river has not been established. Each range has a known cause that data could narrow: the unknown restriction rule for the canal, and the unknown position of the water table relative to the river bed for groundwater.
